## 1. Classification rule

A visual is classified against the independently reconstructed grammar:

- **exact realization** — faithfully exposes its declared source, bounded
  application, outcome or blocker, and required trace/boundary for its stated
  scope;
- **partial realization** — depicts several valid roles but omits essential
  declarations, trace, loss, or blocker behavior;
- **artistic interpretation** — uses the roles metaphorically or adds
  unverified universal structure;
- **unrelated** — communicates domain content without instantiating the grammar
  as an accountable source-to-result structure.

“Exact” applies only to the visual's bounded contract. It never means that the
depicted scientific domain is true.

## 4. OPU and Orientation Machine posters

This family includes the supplied:

- `OLS ↔ OPU — From Language to Execution`;
- `OPU — Orientation Processing Unit`;
- `The Orientation Machine`.

**Classification: artistic interpretation with partial realization.**

They correctly distinguish input, selection/reference conditions,
representation, interpretation, and feedback. They also depict the useful
language-versus-processing distinction.

They exceed repository evidence by presenting:

- OPU as a universal named machine or runtime;
- one fixed filter–reference–orientation–lens–projection pipeline;
- interpretation as an internal machine stage;
- universal adaptivity and “meaning out”;
- a general-purpose engine spanning every domain.

The independent grammar supports a bounded Processor, but not OPU as a new
module or universal architecture.

## 5. Orientation Stack, lens, and Leonardo family

This family includes:

- `The Orientation Stack`;
- `The Lens as Transition`;
- `Leonardo Translations — Triptych`;
- `The Observer in the Field`.

**Classification: artistic interpretation.**

They instantiate observer, perspective, selection, projection,
representation, and interpretation motifs. The triptych is a useful
pedagogical comparison of source, situated observer, and relational image.

They do not faithfully realize the full grammar because they generally omit:

- exact source and version;
- operator identity and capability;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- explicit loss and prohibited implications;
- failure or blocker conditions;
- the distinction between a physical claim and a visual analogy.

Their double cones, apertures, axes, and projection planes are optional domain
models, not universal grammar elements.

## 6. Coordinate-system, Atlas, and transition-geometry family

This family includes:

- `NEXAH Koordinatensystem — Das Gesamtmodell`;
- `Whiteboard II — The Geometric Map`;
- Atlas role-mapping and transition-corridor posters;
- DERIS/SWITCH/gate/core diagrams.

**Classification: partial realization mixed with artistic research
interpretation.**

The family often supplies a reference domain, roles, positions, paths,
transitions, observation axis, and representation. This makes it a plausible
instance of the optional reference configuration.

It is not exact because the visuals commonly:

- treat role names as if they were mathematical coordinates;
- generalize one corridor geometry across unrelated dynamical systems;
- state transition properties without versioned equations or fixtures;
- conflate graphical cross-section, state classification, and mechanism;
- omit provenance, uncertainty, parameter domain, and falsification boundary.

The independent grammar predicts that such diagrams can emerge from a declared
reference configuration. It does not validate their specific geometry.

## 7. DERIS × HYDRA plots

This family includes state-space trajectories, outgoing-direction comparison,
observation-angle sensitivity, unsupervised pre/post projection, four

They are not exact realizations of the grammar as supplied because the visual

The observation-map family strongly illustrates why Representation must remain

## 8. NTO, calendar, cosmic, and harmonic visuals

**Classification: artistic interpretation.**

Cyclic, spiral, prime, Möbius, solar, and planetary arrangements can
instantiate a coordinate-system specialization only after domain, epoch,
units, ordering, boundaries, mappings, and fixtures are declared.

The current NTO review found no complete profile. Therefore these visuals
cannot be classified as exact or partial NTO realizations merely because they
look calendar-like.

## 9. Physics, duality, and “one structure—many regimes” visuals

This family includes the string-theory overview, duality network,
M-theory/regime poster, and scientific-orientation triptych.

**Classification: unrelated as evidence for the grammar; partial only as
generic representation examples.**

They organize scientific subject matter into boxes, arrows, regimes, and
comparisons. That layout alone does not expose an OLS construction, declared
mapping, provenance chain, loss, or blocker.

Established physical dualities must not be reclassified as orientation
relations merely because both connect descriptions. Conversely, orientation

### 10.9 Batch conclusion

None of these eleven visuals is an exact realization of the full structural
grammar. The strongest candidates are:

1. the Unit Ladder, because it names units and a logarithmic intent;
2. the Lissajous panels, because one parameter ratio and phase are shown;
3. the 24-string note ordering, because the discrete sequence is explicit.

Each could become exact through a small mapping specification and immutable
fixtures. The composite causal, physical, symbolic, or universal claims remain
unsupported.

This batch does not change the independent final assessment. It reinforces the
need to distinguish:

```text
correct local equation
    ≠ declared cross-domain relation
    ≠ validated transformation
    ≠ physical mechanism
```

## 11. Visual-validation conclusion

The visual corpus contains all four classes:

- exact realizations where a frozen textual or executable contract precedes
  the visual;
- partial realizations where structural roles are visible but trace is absent;
- artistic interpretations where metaphors add universal-looking geometry;
- unrelated domain visuals whose arrows and symmetry have no evidential
  bearing on the grammar.

The result supports the reverse-direction principle:

> A real grammar can naturally generate many different visualizations, but
> visually recurring cones, axes, layers, colors, or triptychs do not establish
> that grammar.

The most faithful visuals are not the most visually similar ones. They are the
ones whose identities, mappings, boundaries, losses, and evidence can be traced
to non-visual contracts.
